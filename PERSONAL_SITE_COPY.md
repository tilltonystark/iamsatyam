## About

### The longer version

My mom studied psychology, and growing up I heard a lot of conversations at home about how people think and behave. I was studying computer science when a senior shared a Figma link to a website he’d designed for our college. I clicked it, started exploring, and found a field that connected my interest in people with making things.

At first I was drawn to research. I liked talking to people, watching where they hesitated, and thinking carefully about how to ask a question without steering the answer. Later, agency work gave me room to practice the visual side. I still find it fascinating how much someone can do with type, shapes, color, images, and a thoughtful layout.

My interests have shifted over time. I’ve spent stretches learning research methods, building design systems, and working through product flows. Right now I’m exploring typography, grids, and more expressive visual work. I like that design gives me room to keep moving between those things.

In college, I started a design community because most people around me knew coding well but had little exposure to product design. I organized sessions, invited speakers, and passed the community on. I’ve also run small workshops at work. Sharing what I’ve just learned is one of my favorite ways to understand it better.

## How I approach design

### Introduction

When I open a screen that feels busy, I first ask what someone came there to do. What needs their attention now? What can wait? If everything is equally loud, the next step becomes harder to find.

From there I look at the flow. Can someone tell where they are? Do they understand what happens when they act? Is the amount of reading appropriate for the moment? Sometimes a better order fixes the problem. Sometimes I need to rethink the navigation, the structure of a page, or whether a task belongs in a modal, a panel, or its own view.

Then I get into the visual details. Typography, spacing, and alignment can make information much easier to scan. I like trying directions that feel distinct, as long as they still help someone move through the experience.

### A compact three-part version

**Find the job of the screen.** What decision or action should be easiest here?

**Make the path clear.** Work through priority, navigation, reading effort, and the places someone might hesitate.

**Give it character.** Experiment with type, composition, and details while keeping the experience easy to use.
